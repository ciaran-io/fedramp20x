## 0. Changes in v0.2

Re-reading the official rule text and definitions showed four errors in v0.1:

1. **"Federal customer data" was used too loosely.** The official definition **excludes** account information, service metadata, analytics and telemetry generated by the provider. Section 2 now says which categories are federal customer data, which are metadata, and which are mixed. Metadata still comes into scope through `MAS-CSO-MDI`.
2. **Information resources include people and processes.** Section 4.5 adds staff roles, policies and procedures.
3. **The Beacon mobile app is outside FedRAMP scope.** Software delivered for installation on agency devices and not operated in a shared responsibility model is excluded. P10 now covers only the backend; the app is a supplement item (`MAS-CSO-SUP`).
4. **Billing receives per-tenant usage counts**, which is metadata about federal customer data. C4 moves from "Out" to "Out, conditional". Staff devices are also split into managed administrative endpoints (in) and personal or non-admin devices (out, conditional).

**Source of truth.** Decisions, flows and security categories now live in `mas/mas-record.json` and are rendered to `minimum-assessment-scope.md`. This document keeps the reasoning and narrative; if the two differ, the JSON record wins.

---

### Changes in v0.3 (recovery strategy)

Option C (backup and restore) was chosen over warm standby, pilot light and multi-AZ only. Encrypted backups replicate to a second US region, so a new in-scope resource **P12** (cross-region backup replica storage) and flow **F43** were added. There is no standing environment in the recovery region. Section 6 records the decision; open questions OQ9 and OQ10 in the scope record cover the on-demand recovery environment and data removal from backups.

## 1. Method

`MAS-CSO-IIR` requires the provider to identify every information resource that may touch federal customer data or may affect its confidentiality, integrity or availability. The set identified is the cloud service offering.

Section 2 lists the categories of federal customer data and the preliminary CIA impact of each. Section 3 lists the platforms. Section 4 records a decision and rationale for each platform against the two `MAS-CSO-IIR` questions. Section 5 maps data categories to platforms.

Decision values used:

| Decision | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **In** | Part of the cloud service offering and assessed. |
| **In (3P)** | A third-party information resource in the data path. By the official definition it is not entirely inside the assessed scope, so it is documented under `MAS-CSO-TPR` rather than assessed. |
| **Out** | Neither handles federal data nor can affect its confidentiality, integrity or availability. Rationale must say why. |
| **Out, conditional** | Out only while a stated control holds. If the condition fails, it moves in. |

All impact levels below are **preliminary** and use Low / Moderate / High as working labels. They are confirmed (or changed) when security categories are documented in the information flow step.

## 2. Federal customer data categories

In FedRAMP's definitions, **federal customer data** means the content an agency or its users put into the service. Account information and provider-generated metadata, analytics and telemetry are outside that definition. Read the exact wording in the FedRAMP definitions (FRD) in FedRAMP/rules. Metadata is still pulled into scope by `MAS-CSO-MDI`, so excluding a category from the definition does not take it out of the boundary.

| Status | Categories |
|---|---|
| Federal customer data | D1, D2, D3, D4, D10 |
| Mixed (supplied content plus service-generated records) | D5, D7, D8 |
| Not federal customer data (account information or telemetry), in scope as metadata | D6, D9 |

Several of these are judgment calls (open question OQ7 in the record). Category IDs (D1 to D10) are used throughout the document.

| ID | Category | Examples | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | **Responder contact data** | Names, work email, work phone, optional personal mobile number, notification preferences | Personal numbers are the most personal data in the service. |
| D2 | **Schedules and routing policy** | On-call rotations, overrides, escalation chains, routing rules | Reveals who covers what and when, and how alerts flow. |
| D3 | **Alert metadata** | Alert ID, timestamp, severity, category, source tool, target team, source link | Present in every payload mode. Included in scope per `MAS-CSO-MDI`. |
| D4 | **Alert summaries** | Short title and description in standard payload mode | May describe vulnerabilities, incidents or outages in agency systems. |
| D5 | **Response and audit trail** | Delivery attempts, acknowledgements, reassignments, annotations, admin changes | Also the evidence trail agencies use for audits. |
| D6 | **Tenant and identity data** | Tenant configuration, user accounts, roles, SSO assertions and mappings, session records | Controls who can see or change everything else. |
| D7 | **Integration credentials** | API keys, webhook signing secrets, OAuth tokens for customer tools | Compromise lets an attacker send, suppress or forge alerts. |
| D8 | **Support-derived data** | Screenshots, pasted alert text, ticket contents, call notes from customer support | Created by Orrinwick staff. May contain D1 to D6. |
| D9 | **Operational telemetry about tenants** | Application logs, traces, error reports, usage metrics that reference tenants or alerts | Often forgotten; can leak D1 to D4 if not scrubbed. |
| D10 | **Backups and exports** | Database snapshots, object storage versions, customer-requested audit exports | Same data as above in a different place, often with longer life. |

### 2.1 Preliminary CIA impact by category

| ID | Confidentiality impact if lost | Integrity impact if altered | Availability impact if unavailable | Working level (C / I / A) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | Exposes staff personal numbers and who works in security roles; enables targeting and social engineering. | Wrong numbers send pages to the wrong person or nobody. | Responders cannot be reached; mainly shows up as an alert delivery failure. | Mod / Mod / Mod |
| D2 | Reveals coverage gaps (for example, nobody on call at weekends) to an adversary. | A tampered schedule or rule silently diverts or drops critical alerts. | Escalation cannot run; alerts have no routing target. | Mod / **High-leaning** / Mod |
| D3 | Reveals when and how often an agency detects problems and which tools it uses. | Forged or altered metadata can hide or fabricate incidents. | Loss delays triage and reporting. | Mod / Mod / Mod |
| D4 | Describes weaknesses or incidents in agency systems; direct operational value to an attacker. | Altered text misdirects responders or hides severity. | Responders lose context but can use the source link. | **Mod-High** / Mod / Low |
| D5 | Shows response timelines and gaps; moderate sensitivity. | Altered trail undermines audit and accountability evidence. | Loss harms audit evidence but not live response. | Mod / Mod / Low |
| D6 | Account and role data enable account takeover and impersonation. | Role or SSO mapping changes grant access to others' tenants or alerts. | Nobody can sign in to acknowledge alerts. | Mod / **High-leaning** / Mod |
| D7 | Credential theft lets an attacker forge alerts or read routed content. | Replaced keys let an attacker take over an integration path. | Revoked or lost keys stop alerts reaching Beacon. | **Mod-High** / Mod / Mod |
| D8 | Same exposure as whatever was pasted, in a weaker-controlled system. | Low direct impact. | Low direct impact. | Mod / Low / Low |
| D9 | Leaks D1 to D4 through logs and error reports. | Altered logs weaken forensics. | Loss of telemetry delays detection of platform issues. | Mod / Mod / Low |
| D10 | Same as source data; backups often outlive deletion requests. | Corrupted backups defeat recovery. | Loss prevents recovery from an outage or attack. | Mod / Mod / Mod |

**Observations that shape scope**

- **Availability and integrity matter more here than in a typical SaaS.** Beacon exists to make sure alerts reach people. A silent failure (an alert never routed, or routed to the wrong person) is worse than a confidentiality incident for many customers. The platforms that route, schedule and authenticate are in scope even though they hold little data.
- **D2, D6 and D7 are control data.** They contain little sensitive content but determine whether the rest of the system can be trusted. Anything that can change them (admin tools, CI/CD, identity) is in scope under the "impact" test even if it holds no federal data.
- **D4 and D7 pull the working level up.** Alert summaries and integration credentials are why the working security categories are Moderate rather than Low. Class selection is a separate decision: the official rules do not equate Class C with a FIPS 199 impact level, so the target class in `company-and-offering.md` stays a preliminary judgment.

## 7. Resulting boundary

- **Production service (P1 to P13):** in scope. The mobile app is outside scope (supplement SUP1).
- **Engineering and operations:** E2 to E6 and E8 in scope. E1, E7 and E9 are hosted by other companies and documented as third-party resources. E10 and E11 out, with conditions.
- **Support and corporate IT:** S4 in scope. S1 to S3, C4 and C5 out, with conditions. C1 to C3 and C6 out.
- **People and processes:** N1 to N7 in scope.
- **Third parties:** E1, E7, E9, T1, T2, T3 and T5 documented as third-party resources. T4 out, with a scrubbing condition.
- **Totals:** 27 in scope (20 machine-based, 7 people and process), 7 third party, 8 conditional, 4 out, 1 supplement.

The boundary covers whatever can hold alert, contact or credential data, and whatever can change routing, identity or deployed code. Every conditional exclusion names the control that keeps it out.
